\paragraph{PIC and modular document caches.}
CacheBlend, TurboRAG, RAGCache, and Cache-Craft precompute or reuse retrieved
chunk KV and handle context-dependent composition in different ways
~\citep{cacheblend,turborag,ragcache,cachecraft}. PIC systems such as EPIC and
MEPIC splice independently encoded per-layer KV and repair boundaries or
selected tokens; APE uses parallel context encoding and attention realignment
~\citep{epic,mepic,ape}. Fusion RAG Cache studies selection and cache fusion at
serving scale~\citep{fusionrag}. Learned modular objects further include KV
Packet's independently compiled KV plus boundary adapters and Cartridges'
distilled reusable KV representations~\citep{kvpacket,cartridgesbase}.
Concurrent Cartridges at Scale and SemPIC retrieve learned per-document KV
objects~\citep{cartridges,sempic}. These works already establish reusable
document caches. CoMem does \emph{not} claim that idea; it studies the distinct
single-residual object, whose split depth trades persistent bytes and online
upper-layer computation. We lack a matched same-backbone implementation of
these systems, so Table~\ref{tab:priorart} supports taxonomy rather than
superiority. No artifact in this study supplies a same-backbone, same-hardware
PIC, chunk-KV-repair, or learned modular-cache result under our pack, storage
tier, and timing boundary.

\paragraph{Intermediate-state reuse and token reduction.}
ReadOnce Transformers and Embedding Recycling cache intermediate text
representations and adapt later layers~\citep{readonce,embeddingrecycling}.
HCache checkpoints activations to restore evicted state; KV-Direct reconstructs
layer-wise KV from residuals; ILRe and REFORM select or gather tokens before
recomputation~\citep{hcache,kvdirect,ilre,reform}. GemFilter likewise selects
with early layers but does not persist the states across queries
~\citep{gemfilter}. Token/KV compression instead changes the retained token set
or state budget after full or partial processing
~\citep{streamingllm,h2o,snapkv,pyramidkv,minicache}. CoMem's narrow technical
object is one persistent depth-$j$ residual per selected token followed by
direct execution of $[j{:}L)$.

\paragraph{External memory and the matched endpoint.}
MemoryLLM, LongMem, and XC-Cache add latent pools or auxiliary readers
~\citep{memoryllm,longmem,xccache}; they are useful external references but not
matched depth controls. Our $j{=}0$ endpoint is deliberately narrower than
``RAG'': it fixes selector, chunk IDs and order, sink, mask, examples, and
adapter, then replaces stored residuals with token IDs. The paper's causal claim
is therefore about depth reuse under identical evidence, not a ranking over
retrieval or memory systems.

\paragraph{Scope of the contribution.}
Caching intermediate representations and reusable document state both predate
CoMem. Our empirical contribution is narrower: one residual per token at a
chosen split, direct suffix execution on a bounded selected pack, and a
controlled $j{=}0$ endpoint with explicit Write/Read/storage accounting. We
therefore position the paper as an internal measurement and diagnosis of this
design point, pending matched comparison with the nearest reusable-context
systems.
