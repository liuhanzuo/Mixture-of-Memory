\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Models and unified protocol.} Unless noted, the backbone is the
\textbf{Qwen3-8B base LM}~\citep{qwen3} (36 layers), continued-trained without
SFT or RL. All methods are evaluated under a unified plain-text prompting
protocol, with chat templating disabled (\texttt{chat\_template=False}). The
backbone remains frozen; the flagship trains only our self-distilled rank-32
LoRA at split $j{=}12$ and uses chunk size 512, a BOS sink, and
\texttt{iter\_bm25}. All five benchmark supports fix top-$12$, hop width four,
and \texttt{rounds=0}, which automatically executes three retrieval rounds; the
observed read is approximately 6.5k tokens. The native window is 40,960 tokens;
reaching the configured 131,072-token limit requires YaRN~\citep{yarn}.

\paragraph{Baselines and position range.} KV-Direct~\citep{kvdirect}, the
HCache-style reproduction~\citep{hcache}, StreamingLLM~\citep{streamingllm}, and
InfLLM~\citep{infllm} use Qwen3-8B. We report KV-Direct with native RoPE and with
YaRN factor 4. Native-RoPE cells above 40,960 tokens are unextended stress
references; the YaRN arm is the same-backbone length-extension comparison. For
MemoryLLM~\citep{memoryllm}, we use its released Llama-3-8B-chat
backbone~\citep{llama3} as an out-of-backbone diagnostic. Its official chat
template does not improve the evaluated supports
(Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}).

\paragraph{Evaluation \& metrics.} We evaluate three RULER
families~\citep{ruler}, BABILong qa1/qa2/qa5~\citep{babilong}, and six LongBench
QA datasets~\citep{longbench}, naming these subsets explicitly. RULER and
BABILong tables contain 100 examples per task--length cell except the separate
CoMem+YaRN row, which uses 50. RULER uses official
\texttt{string\_match\_all}; BABILong uses \texttt{compare\_answers} with
\texttt{TASK\_LABELS} and never \texttt{re.search}. LongBench and
LoCoMo~\citep{locomo} use \texttt{run\_scoring}. LongEval follows the LongChat
lines-retrieval protocol~\citep{longchat}. LoCoMo reports deterministic token F1
and substring accuracy as lexical diagnostics, with semantic Judge as the
primary metric: GPT-4o scores categories 1--4, while category 5 uses the official
local abstention rule. Complete RULER grids passed the eight-shard completeness
check, contained no empty predictions, and matched scores recomputed from disk.
Appendix~\ref{app:statistics} gives aggregation and uncertainty details.

\paragraph{Hardware.} Headline Qwen3-8B timing uses one NVIDIA L20A with the
flagship LoRA enabled (median of three runs, chunk size 512). At 128k,
full-context prefill peaks at 89.39\,GB and CoMem at 18.54\,GB. A separate
same-H20 split-depth sweep holds the retrieved pack fixed and is compared only
within that cohort. Large-MoE details are in Appendix~\ref{app:generality}.

\subsection{Core benchmark overview}
Table~\ref{tab:overview} compares five audited benchmark supports under the
unified protocol. CoMem scores 97.05 on the 15-cell RULER subset and 69.0 on
LongEval versus 78.80/65.2 for unextended KV-Direct, while matching its
six-dataset LongBench QA macro F1 (12.15 vs.\ 12.17). These aggregate differences
partly reflect cells beyond the native window. The same-backbone length-extension comparison in Table~\ref{tab:scaling} shows
the sharper trade-off: at 128k, CoMem+LoRA without YaRN ($n{=}50$) versus
KV-Direct+YaRN ($n{=}100$) scores 98/96/98.4 versus 100/89/57.8 on single
needle, multikey, and variable tracking. Enabling YaRN on CoMem gives
96/90/87.6, so position scaling is not uniformly helpful. The mixed-$n$
comparison is descriptive rather than paired. Within-window BABILong still
exposes a qa1/qa2 compression tax, whereas CoMem leads qa5. The contribution is
a bounded-read trade-off, not uniform accuracy dominance.

\subsection{Equal-budget comparison}
At the same $\sim$6.5k-token budget, CoMem stays near 100 on single needles and
above 95 on variable tracking through 128k, while StreamingLLM falls
85$\to$2 and 41.8$\to$0.8 (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:slm}). With read length
approximately fixed, the gap shows that relevant rather than merely recent
chunks are essential.

\subsection{Long-dialogue memory}

CoMem scores 38.27 versus 34.59 for KV-Direct on all 1,986 LoCoMo questions.
The advantage is not an artifact of one scorer or treating correlated questions
as independent: on 1,540 paired answerable items, the $+4.81$ gap has
item-bootstrap 95\% CI $[2.34,7.27]$; a 10-conversation cluster bootstrap gives
$[1.27,8.32]$ ($p{\approx}0.004$; 8/10 conversations favor CoMem); and a
stratified 200-item DeepSeek-V3 audit preserves the ordering
($\kappa=0.626$ with GPT-4o). Retrieved $j=0$
reaches 41.59, showing that retrieval contributes independently of depth reuse,
while the same-$j$ adapter control below measures how much fidelity is recovered.
Appendix~\ref{app:statistics} details both uncertainty analyses and judge prompts.

\subsection{End-to-end efficiency}

Table~\ref{tab:eff} uses one platform and the actual distilled flagship. Prefill
crosses parity between 16k and 32k and reaches $2.74\times$ at 128k, where peak
memory is 18.54\,GB versus 89.39\,GB. LoRA-on cached decode costs about
31--32\,ms/token versus 22\,ms/token for Dense but is independent of stored
context for a fixed pack. We do not mix an older $7.83\times$ online-query
cohort, which used a persistent-index timing convention, with the full-ingest
component measurements.

The separate fixed-pack sweep (Table~\ref{tab:pareto}) makes the text-retrieval
baseline explicit. Moving from retrieved full recomputation ($j{=}0$) to
$j{=}12$ lowers online Read from 1.01 to 0.72\,s but increases measured offline
Write from 0.23 to 7.79\,s, implying a component-wise break-even near 26 repeated
queries. CoMem stores 8\,KB/token (about 1\,GiB at 128k), far more than text or
token IDs. Depth caching is therefore a high-query-reuse operating point, not a
free replacement for text retrieval. Appendix~\ref{app:efficiency} gives the
measurement protocol and remaining systems limitations.

\subsection{What drives the result?}
\paragraph{Retrieval versus depth reuse.}
The controlled depth sweep (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:depth-tradeoff}) holds the
retrieved pack fixed. The $j=0$ row performs full-depth recomputation on it and
therefore isolates selection from depth reuse. It reaches 41.59 on LoCoMo,
compared with 34.59 for full-context KV-Direct, showing that bounded selection
can improve natural dialogue memory even before layer reuse. Deeper frozen
splits reduce quality (32.78/29.15/24.52 at $j=6/9/12$) as query-blind states
become harder to read, but a same-pack 128k sweep reduces Read from 1.01 to
0.72\,s. Depth is therefore a quality--query-cost knob rather than a free
accuracy gain.

\paragraph{Self-distillation repairs readout fidelity.}
At the same $j=12$, the PG19-only LoRA restores 13.75 LoCoMo points and
22.2/9.0/8.4 BABILong qa1/qa2/qa5 points. The controlled RULER cohort similarly
moves single-needle recall from 35/5/17 to 100/100/100 and multikey recall from
4/2/1 to 91/95/92 over 8k/16k/32k (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:adapter}). This
supports adaptation of the split interface rather than benchmark-specific
training: PG19 contains no retrieval or task supervision.

\paragraph{Selection breadth and chunk interaction.}

Oracle retrieval keeps the reported needle cells at 100, whereas recency and
reader-attention selectors degrade with length (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:selector}). More retrieval is not automatically better: the
$\sim$17k four-hop variant is worse than the focused $\sim$6.5k flagship from
16k onward on variable tracking (Table~\ref{tab:itervt}), consistent with
additional distractor chains. Once selected, chunks must interact: replacing
full cross-chunk attention by block-diagonal reuse costs 36/62 points on
8k/16k multikey retrieval and 19/7 points on BABILong qa2
(Table~\ref{tab:crosschunk}). Together these controls justify bounded relevance
selection, a tunable split, full cross-chunk recomputation, and lightweight
interface adaptation as distinct parts of CoMem.

\subsection{Additional tasks and generality}
Appendix~\ref{app:tasks} gives the remaining task details. Across Qwen3
0.6B--32B, the specified linear probe places content depth near $0.45L$ while
the zero-shot readable boundary moves deeper with scale; this is a
probe-dependent trend, not a causal localization or a claim of complete task
transfer at every size. Three LoRA seeds evaluated on identical cells and sample counts yield a
median cell-wise standard deviation of 1.34 points and a maximum of 4.36 over 18
reported RULER/BABILong cells (Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}). CoMem is also viable on two sparse MoE
backbones. On Hy3, it keeps a $\approx$4.3--4.6k-token read through 256k and
reaches 100/98 single/multikey recall (Appendix~\ref{app:generality}).
