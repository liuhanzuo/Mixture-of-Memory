\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.}
The principal model is frozen Qwen3-8B~\citep{qwen3}, with $j{=}12$,
512-token chunks, iterative BM25 top-12, a BOS sink, and the rank-32 LoRA.
Prompting is plain text without chat templates. We evaluate RULER
~\citep{ruler}, BABILong~\citep{babilong}, LongEval~\citep{longchat}, six
LongBench QA datasets~\citep{longbench}, and all 1,986 LoCoMo questions
~\citep{locomo}. Appendices~\ref{app:tasks} and~\ref{app:statistics} give
complete cells, prompts, sample counts, uncertainty, and integrity checks. We keep four
timing boundaries separate: selected-pack model Read, store-ready online
prefill, Write-inclusive pipeline, and external index/store cost.

\subsection{Matched depth reuse}
\input{sections/tab_core_tradeoff}

Table~\ref{tab:core-tradeoff} is the central comparison. Raw-text replay
($j{=}0$) and CoMem receive the same selected chunks in the same order and use
the same sink, mask, examples, and LoRA. Skipping the lower 12 layers reduces
fixed-pack Read from 931.9 to 664.4\,ms ($1.403\times$), but lowers the RULER
macro from 99.19 to 96.07. This gives the matched depth frontier: $1.403\times$ faster Read for a
3.12-point quality cost. The continuous-prefix control exactly matches $j{=}0$,
showing that the upper 24 layers retain sufficient capacity to reproduce replay
when supplied compatible states. It therefore turns the observed loss into a
Write-interface problem that the following controls can diagnose.

Natural-task transfer is uneven. Relative to matched $j{=}0$, $j{=}12$
falls sharply on LongEval (97.2 to 69.0), changes modestly on LoCoMo (41.59 to
38.27), and is nearly unchanged on six LongBench QA datasets (12.31 to 12.15;
Table~\ref{tab:overview}); BABILong varies by task
(Table~\ref{tab:babilong}). The detailed tables
(Tables~\ref{tab:longeval}, \ref{tab:longbench}, and \ref{tab:locomo}) therefore
support transfer of the interface, not uniform preservation of task quality.
Data-overlap and judge qualifications appear in the appendix and Limitations.

\subsection{Same-backbone chunk-KV comparison}
\input{sections/tab_cacheblend_baseline}

Table~\ref{tab:cacheblend-baseline} compares CoMem with a minimal faithful
CacheBlend-style arm~\citep{cacheblend} on the same Qwen3-8B backbone,
iterative-BM25 top-12 evidence, 512-token chunks, plain-text prompts, and
scorers. The baseline independently prefills full-depth per-chunk KV, reindexes
RoPE when chunks are assembled, and selectively recomputes the highest-deviation
context tokens; a real-model $r{=}1$ self-test agrees with vanilla full prefill
to $2.8\!\times\!10^{-5}$ maximum logit difference and 100\% top-1 agreement.
Increasing $r$ from 0 to .18 improves its 12-cell RULER macro from 67.80 to
74.70, but leaves BABILong qa5 near 49 and LoCoMo substring accuracy near 17.
CoMem reaches 97.05, 68.67, and 23.36, respectively, while its single residual
uses 8\,KiB/token rather than 144\,KiB/token for full-depth bf16 GQA KV. Thus,
on this matched evidence path, retaining every layer's independently prepared
KV does not recover the query-conditioned quality that CoMem obtains by storing
one residual and recomputing the upper 24 layers. This is an implementation-level
comparison rather than a reproduction of CacheBlend's complete native serving
stack; the baseline is training-free, whereas CoMem uses its distilled adapter.

\subsection{Amortization and equal-latency boundaries}
\input{sections/tab_serving_crossover}

The residual store costs 8,192 bytes/token and must first be written. In the
write-once serving harness, CPU-pinned break-even is 8.9--10.9 queries at 32k
for generated lengths up to 128 tokens; the full GPU/CPU grid is 5.5--10.9.
The 512-token 32k CPU case moves to 94 queries because decode dominates, and
the 32k GPU cell has no finite crossover at that output length. At 128k,
break-even is 25.8--27.2 queries for GPU placement and 27.6--37.4 for CPU
placement through $G{=}128$; at 1M tokens it rises to roughly 180--421 queries.
Each value follows the displayed measured-time equation and includes fetch,
model Read, and decode; for one-off or weakly reused documents, raw text is
cheaper.

\input{sections/tab_equal_latency}

We then ask whether cheaper replay can buy enough extra evidence to improve
quality at equal online latency. A latency-only calibration freezes maximum
budgets of 10 replay chunks and 12 CoMem chunks; selectors may under-fill.
With the same iterative-BM25 ranking, raw replay
leads by 11.56 points. Replacing only the replay selector with frozen BGE changes
the point difference to $-1.00$, while the unchanged CoMem arm remains at
53.22. A hierarchical bootstrap that first resamples cells gives
$[-18.67,-5.11]$ for BM25 and $[-10.67,8.33]$ for BGE; leave-one-cell-out
ranges are $[-13.13,-9.50]$ and $[-3.50,1.75]$. The decision-relevant conclusion is selector dependence: BM25 replay has a
robust advantage, whereas the BGE aggregate is unresolved under task-cell
dependence. Depth reuse can buy a larger evidence budget, but whether that
budget closes the representation gap depends strongly on retrieval quality.
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:equal-latency-protocol} exposes the cohort, weights,
generation limits, split, absolute latencies, and bootstrap unit.

\subsection{Write-interface diagnosis and repair}
\input{sections/tab_context_position}
\input{sections/tab_write_context}

Holding retrieval, adapter, and upper-layer Read fixed, the $2{\times}2$
diagnostic separates lower-layer attention scope from Read positions.
Document-contextual Write closes the gap under either position convention;
document-origin positions alone make chunk-local states 4.5 points worse. The $+4.5$ interaction precludes a purely additive explanation and identifies
the Write representation---especially missing lower-layer document context---as
the dominant tested source of the gap rather than position remapping.

Overlap-Write turns this control into a local deployable intervention:
$w{=}32$ raises 92.5 to 98.5 and $w{=}128$ to 99.0 while preserving stored
bytes and per-query work. Its measured wall-clock overhead exceeds the theoretical FLOP ratios, but the
result demonstrates that most of the local gap can be repaired with only 32
left-context tokens and no increase in persistent bytes or online Read.

\subsection{Efficiency and timing boundaries}
The efficiency results deliberately answer three different questions. In the
store-ready H20 cohort, stock Qwen3-8B without the CoMem adapter prefills the
entire 128k source in 71.37\,s, whereas CoMem processes a fixed 6,657-token
selected pack with its adapter in 1.10\,s; this is the composed
$64.9\times$ online-prefill point and excludes one-time document Write and
external-store fetch (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:online-prefill}). A second H20
Pareto cohort reports 50.59\,s for the same stock dense path under a different
run and harness and 0.807--1.32\,s retrieved operating points
(Table~\ref{tab:pareto}); it is not the denominator of the $64.9\times$ claim.
Finally, the same-adapter L20A pipeline comparison processes the full source in
6.035\,s and CoMem in 2.202\,s ($2.74\times$), with the latter including
document Write, CPU BM25 retrieval, query Write, and upper-layer Read but
excluding BM25-index construction and external I/O (Table~\ref{tab:eff}). The
three measurements therefore separate store-ready online work, a representative
H20 Pareto cohort, and a Write-inclusive same-adapter pipeline rather than
forming interchangeable speedup denominators.

The matched $j{=}0\!\rightarrow\!12$ result has a still narrower boundary: it
saves 267.5\,ms of selected-pack Read at 16k (931.9 to 664.4\,ms). In that
same benchmark, total autoregressive decode medians are approximately
2.76--2.86\,s for both arms, so combining Read and decode yields only about a
$1.07$--$1.09\times$ ratio, depending on which arm's observed decode median is
paired with its Read. This does not contradict the $64.9\times$ 128k prefill
point: the former asks how much skipping 12 layers changes a fixed-pack
request once generation dominates, whereas the latter asks how bounded
selection plus depth reuse changes prefill before generation. Autoregressive
decode is therefore the remaining bottleneck for short selected packs, as in
other bounded-read systems; speculative decoding and prompt/prefix-cache reuse
are orthogonal, but we do not measure their composition.
