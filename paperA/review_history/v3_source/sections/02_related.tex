\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We position CoMem by the object that is stored, the scope over which it can be
reused, and the computation performed after retrieval. Table~\ref{tab:priorart}
summarizes representative interfaces; it is not an empirical ranking.

\paragraph{Token-axis KV reduction.}
StreamingLLM retains a recent window and attention sinks
~\citep{streamingllm}; H2O and SnapKV evict positions
~\citep{h2o,snapkv}; PyramidKV, ZigZagKV, LCKV, and MiniCache redistribute,
condense, or merge layer-wise states~\citep{pyramidkv,zigzagkv,lckv,minicache}.
Query-aware systems such as Quest, RetrievalAttention, ACRE, FIER, A$^2$ATS,
HeteroCache, and REAL dynamically retrieve or retain relevant KV
~\citep{quest,retrievalattention,acre,fier,a2ats,heterocache,real}. These methods
primarily optimize which \emph{tokens or KV entries} remain available. CoMem
instead stores one intermediate residual per token and reconstructs the selected
tokens' upper-layer computation. This depth partition is complementary to
quantization, eviction, and heterogeneous storage.

\paragraph{Intermediate activations and recomputation.}
HCache checkpoints intermediate activations to restore evicted execution state
~\citep{hcache}. KV-Direct reconstructs layer-wise KV from residuals but keeps
the full token sequence~\citep{kvdirect}. ILRe uses intermediate-layer
representations to retrieve tokens for context compression~\citep{ilre}.
REFORM incrementally compresses context, gathers salient tokens, and recomputes
their KV~\citep{reform}. These works make intermediate states a useful systems
object. CoMem differs in the conjunction of \emph{cross-query persistence},
\emph{independently written chunks}, \emph{bounded query-time selection}, and
\emph{direct continuation from the corresponding depth}. We restrict our
novelty claim to that interface combination.

\paragraph{Reusable context and chunk caches.}
CacheBlend, RAGCache, IMDC, and Cache-Craft reuse or repair cached states for
retrieval-augmented serving~\citep{cacheblend,ragcache,imdc,cachecraft}.
Cache-Craft is particularly close in workload: retrieved chunks recur across
queries, but arbitrary context changes make their KV caches only partially
reusable. It selectively recomputes cache regions to restore quality. CoMem
stores a single residual tensor rather than layer-wise chunk KV and exposes
split depth as an explicit reuse knob; our overlap-Write variant analogously
shows that limited neighboring context can repair independently cached chunks.

\paragraph{Learned and architectural memory.}
MemoryLLM maintains a fixed-size latent pool, LongMem retrieves external states
through a side network, and RecursiveSummarizing stores natural-language
summaries~\citep{memoryllm,longmem,recsum}. YOCO and CEPE redesign the model to
share or encode context efficiently~\citep{yoco,cepe}; Activation Beacon,
KV-CAT, LLoCO, and IndexMem train compressible or compensating memory
representations~\citep{actbeacon,kvcat,lloco,indexmem}. CoMem keeps the backbone
frozen and trains only an optional interface LoRA. Its residual store grows
linearly with the written corpus and is therefore not a constant-capacity memory.

\paragraph{Raw-text retrieval.}
Conventional RAG retrieves text and recomputes the reader
~\citep{rag,xu2024retrievallong}. Our $j{=}0$ baseline is not intended to
represent every RAG system: it fixes CoMem's selector, selected chunk order,
sink, and attention mask, making it the direct endpoint of the depth axis.
Dense-retriever experiments in the appendix show that realistic retrieval can
introduce a separate recall bottleneck. The central comparison in this paper is
therefore not ``memory versus RAG'' in general, but raw-text full-depth replay
versus residual upper-layer continuation under the same evidence pack.
