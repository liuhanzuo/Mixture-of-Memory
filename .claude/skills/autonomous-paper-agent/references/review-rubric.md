# Generic Research-Paper Review Rubric

Use an official venue rubric when available. This generic rubric is an internal quality-control fallback.

## Overall Score: 1-10

- **10 — Landmark:** extraordinarily strong, field-shaping, technically and empirically definitive; extremely rare.
- **9 — Exceptional:** major contribution with compelling evidence and no material unresolved weakness.
- **8 — Strong accept:** clear, important contribution; strong evidence; only limited, non-decision-changing weaknesses.
- **7 — Accept:** solid contribution with convincing core evidence; remaining weaknesses are addressable and do not undermine the main conclusion.
- **6 — Weak accept / positive borderline:** useful and mostly sound, but novelty, evidence, clarity, or scope leaves meaningful uncertainty.
- **5 — Weak reject / negative borderline:** promising, but at least one material issue prevents a positive decision in the current form.
- **4 — Reject:** major technical, empirical, novelty, or reproducibility problems.
- **3 — Clear reject:** central claims are weakly supported, substantially incorrect, or poorly positioned.
- **2 — Fundamentally flawed:** central method or evidence is invalid or impossible to assess.
- **1 — Unreviewable / invalid:** pervasive integrity, coherence, or evidence failure.

## Confidence: 1-5

- **5:** expert in the exact area; checked all central technical and empirical details.
- **4:** strong familiarity; minor uncertainty does not affect the main judgment.
- **3:** adequate familiarity; some material uncertainty remains.
- **2:** limited familiarity or incomplete evidence access.
- **1:** highly uncertain; score should have little weight without adjudication.

## Dimension Scores: 1-5

### Novelty

1. No identifiable distinction or clear duplication.
2. Mostly incremental or novelty claim unsupported.
3. Some meaningful distinction, but boundary is unclear or narrow.
4. Clear and useful novelty relative to closest work.
5. Substantial conceptual or methodological advance.

### Significance

1. Trivial or irrelevant problem/contribution.
2. Limited value or impact.
3. Useful to a meaningful niche.
4. Broadly important or practically consequential.
5. Potentially field-shaping.

### Technical Soundness

1. Central argument invalid.
2. Serious unresolved correctness problems.
3. Mostly plausible with notable gaps.
4. Correct and well justified with minor gaps.
5. Exceptionally rigorous and complete.

### Experimental Rigor

1. Evidence does not test the claims.
2. Major design, leakage, baseline, or statistical problems.
3. Reasonable evidence with important missing controls or uncertainty.
4. Strong, fair, well-controlled evaluation.
5. Decisive and unusually comprehensive evidence.

### Clarity

1. Incoherent or unreviewable.
2. Major organization or exposition barriers.
3. Understandable but uneven or unnecessarily difficult.
4. Clear, well organized, and effective.
5. Exceptionally precise and persuasive.

### Reproducibility

1. Core result cannot be traced or reproduced.
2. Major missing implementation/evaluation details.
3. Partial reproducibility with meaningful gaps.
4. Core results are well specified and reproducible.
5. Exceptional provenance, artifacts, and verification.

### Citation Integrity

1. Fabricated, irrelevant, or materially misleading citations.
2. Significant missing or unsupported attribution.
3. Mostly adequate with localized gaps.
4. Accurate, well targeted, and verified.
5. Exemplary coverage and claim-level support.

### Limitations and Responsible Claims

1. Material risks or limitations hidden; conclusions grossly overstate evidence.
2. Serious overclaiming or inadequate limitation analysis.
3. Mostly responsible with some scope ambiguity.
4. Claims are appropriately bounded and limitations are clear.
5. Exceptionally careful scope, uncertainty, and risk treatment.

## Internal Quality Index

When an official venue does not specify weights, compute an internal 0-10 quality index as:

- novelty: 15%
- significance: 15%
- technical soundness: 20%
- experimental rigor: 20%
- clarity: 10%
- reproducibility: 10%
- citation integrity: 5%
- limitations and responsible claims: 5%

Convert the weighted 1-5 mean to 0-10 by multiplying by 2.

This index is diagnostic only. It must not replace the reviewer's holistic overall score or a venue's official decision process.

## Decision-Relevant Questions

Every reviewer should answer:

1. What is the strongest verified contribution?
2. What is the most severe unresolved issue?
3. Which claim has the weakest evidence-to-importance ratio?
4. What is the paper's score ceiling without new experiments?
5. What single change would most improve confidence?
6. What evidence would cause the score to decrease?
