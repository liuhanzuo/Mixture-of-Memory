## 头部候选详情

### `task-surface-4` — The Missing Column: Multi-Span and Random-Span Infilling as Diffusion's Uncontested Home Turf

- **可行性** ready-now / 18 GPU-h · **overlap** strong-overlap-differentiable · **建议** pursue-as-followup

**Claim**: Diffusion's advantage over AR-FIM grows with the number of simultaneous holes. On a k-span infilling benchmark (k=1,2,4 non-contiguous holes), AR-FIM degrades sharply because it must serialise holes and cannot condition each hole on the others' eventual contents, whereas masked diffusion fills all k jointly in one bidirectional process. If true, k-span infilling — not from-scratch generation — is the correct headline task for diffusion code models.

**差异化定位**:

> The candidate's premise needs one correction that actually strengthens it: the k-span code-infilling benchmark is NOT unbuilt. Mündler, Dekoninck & Vechev (arXiv:2508.10111, v1 2025-08-13, ICLR 2026 Poster) named the paradigm "multi-region infilling (mri)", constructed it on HumanEval by removing random spans following Bavarian et al., and published a clean 1-mri/2-mri/3-mri ladder showing exactly the monotone AR collapse the candidate predicts (DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B functional 64.8 -> 29.8 -> 11.9; syntax 93.9 -> 62.0 -> 32.9 across five AR models). Our zero-hit searches for "multi-span" missed it purely on terminology; "multi-region infilling" returns it as the single hit. This does not kill the candidate — it converts it from a benchmark-construction paper into a much better-defended interaction paper, because the one thing 2508.10111 never does is run a diffusion model on mri: its diffusion section (LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B, DreamCoder-7B, DiffuCoder-7B at 32 steps) evaluates FROM-SCRATCH C++/JSON/SMILES generation, so the diffusion x k cell — the entire content of our headline — is empty, and no paper anywhere fills it. Our differentiation is therefore fourfold and each part is independently sufficient. (i) Family: we supply the missing diffusion column on a k-ladder, which is the first measurement of the k x family interaction; every diffusion infilling paper to date is single-hole by construction (DreamOn's released harness literally hardcodes read_problems(benchmark_name='single-line') at vendor/DreamOn/eval/evaluate.py:95, verified in our checkout; CAL reports zero hits for "random span"). (ii) Protocol fairness: 2508.10111's AR arm at k>1 uses natural-language "// region 1 / // region 2" markers inside a chat prompt with a stub main(), never the models' native FIM sentinels, and never varies serialisation order — so its "AR degrades with k" is confounded with prompt-format brittleness. InCoder's canonical multi-mask protocol (Appendix B.2) fills masks strictly in order, and nobody has ever tested whether order matters. We run three AR conditions (native-FIM sequential, native-FIM permuted, chat region-marker) so the serialisation penalty is separated from the format penalty. (iii) Cost accounting: no paper on this axis reports quality against tokens — 2508.10111 reports only constrained-decoding overhead, DreamOn sweeps mask length 4..64 with no cost column, Set Diffusion reports diffusion throughput as an explicit lower bound. We report pass@1 versus cumulative_model_tokens, which is where our repo's three retractions taught us the real claims live. (iv) Topology vs length: Set Diffusion's "Infill k/5" (concurrent, ICML 2026) varies the LENGTH of one contiguous middle, not the NUMBER of disjoint holes; we hold total masked tokens fixed while varying k, so topology is isolated from the length-elasticity axis that DreamOn and CAL already own. Setting differences add further separation: Python + evalplus untrusted_check (2508.10111 is C++-only, with 3-mri functional pinned at 4.9-12.6% i.e. near-floor with almost no dynamic range) and a 256-token abort that conflates capability with budget truncation.

**收紧后的 claim**:

> Reposition from "we build the missing k-span benchmark" to "we measure the k x family interaction that the paper which built the k-span benchmark left empty, on a protocol that survives the fairness objections its AR arm does not." Concretely: Mündler et al. (arXiv:2508.10111, ICLR 2026) established multi-region infilling (mri) and showed AR code models collapse monotonically with hole count k on HumanEval (DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B pass@1 64.8 -> 29.8 -> 11.9 for k=1,2,3), but evaluated diffusion LLMs only on from-scratch generation, leaving the diffusion x k cell empty; no paper has filled it. We measure it on a Python, evalplus-graded, AST-guided ladder with k in {1,2,4} CROSSED with total-masked-tokens held constant (separating hole topology from the span-length axis that DreamOn and CAL already own), reporting pass@1 against cumulative_model_tokens with per-cell truncation rates disclosed. The AR arm is run in three protocols — native-FIM sentinel serialisation in InCoder order, native-FIM with permuted hole order, and the chat region-marker format used by 2508.10111 — so an intrinsic serialisation cost is distinguished from prompt-format brittleness and from ordering choice. Primary statistic: the k x family interaction on the quality-compute plane. Three outcomes are all publishable and pre-registered: diffusion's advantage grows with k (the paradigm's structural claim finally has evidence, and k-span infilling, not from-scratch generation, is the right headline task for diffusion code models); the interaction is null (diffusion's parallel-fill advantage is nominal, not realised — and the field's belief to the contrary rests on a single CoRR-only paper, arXiv:2510.04605, that compares a diffusion code model against a general-purpose AR model); or AR's apparent k-collapse largely disappears under native-FIM serialisation with permuted order, in which case the published AR degradation is a prompting artifact and the correct contribution is that repair.

### `capability-1` — Joint Multi-Hole Infilling: the one thing FIM structurally cannot do

- **可行性** needs-small-build / 12 GPU-h · **overlap** partial-overlap · **建议** pursue-differentiated

**Claim**: When two or more DISJOINT code holes are mutually dependent (a variable defined in hole A and consumed in hole B), filling them JOINTLY in one bidirectional denoising process beats filling them sequentially with the same model, and beats a matched autoregressive coder using FIM; the advantage grows with the number of holes k and with def-use distance. Corollary: multi-hole coupled infilling is the first task surface where a 7B dLLM strictly dominates a matched 7B AR code model at equal token cost.

**差异化定位**:

> No paper before 2026-05-07 measures, with an execution-based metric, whether a masked diffusion code LM benefits from filling MUTUALLY DEPENDENT holes JOINTLY rather than SEQUENTIALLY, at matched model, matched weights, and matched token cost. Four bodies of work bracket this gap without closing it. (1) Melcer et al. (2508.10111) builds precisely the k-in-{1,2,3}-region HumanEval infilling ladder we propose and shows AR pass@1 collapsing monotonically with k (DeepSeek-Coder-33B: 69.8 -> 29.8 -> 12.6), but its regions are RANDOM rather than def-use-coupled, and -- decisively -- it runs the k-mri ladder only on AR FIM models, while its diffusion section evaluates full C++/JSON/SMILES generation instead; so the diffusion-vs-AR cell on multi-region infilling is empty in the very paper that named the task. (2) InCoder (ICLR 2023) and the GLM/CM3 lineage do support multi-region infilling in the OBJECTIVE and even claim 'multiple dependent regions', but their only functionally graded infilling evals are single contiguous spans, their multi-region evidence is a qualitative figure plus an exact-match renaming benchmark, and their generation is still serialised region-by-region -- i.e. they instantiate our SEQUENTIAL control and never contrast it with anything joint. (3) DreamOn (ICLR 2026 Poster) and CAL are strictly single-middle: a full-text grep of DreamOn for 'multi-span', 'disjoint', 'multiple spans' returns zero hits, and 'multi-line' denotes one contiguous multi-line middle. **收紧后的 claim**:

> On code holes that are MUTUALLY DEPENDENT by def-use analysis, we measure -- for the first time with an execution-based metric and at matched token cost -- whether filling k holes JOINTLY in one bidirectional denoising pass beats filling them SEQUENTIALLY with identical weights, thereby supplying the diffusion row that arXiv:2508.10111 left empty in its own 1/2/3-region HumanEval infilling ladder. ### `capability-5` — Coupled Multi-Site Repair: parallel commits where an autoregressive editor must serialize

- **可行性** needs-small-build / 10 GPU-h · **overlap** partial-overlap · **建议** pursue-differentiated

**Claim**: On bugs whose fix requires mutually consistent changes at two or more disjoint sites (rename at definition and all uses; signature change and all call sites), a masked diffusion model that commits all sites in one denoising pass achieves higher consistency and test pass rate than the same model editing site-by-site, and than a matched AR code model editing site-by-site -- but we test it against the strongest AR arm, whole-function rewrite, which sees every site at once.

**差异化定位**:

(1) The multi-hunk repair literature (2506.04418 HUNK4J, 2511.11012, 2607.26591 MultiFixer, 2605.06209 SiblingRepair) is entirely autoregressive -- agents, spectrum-based localization, multi-agent coordinators -- and is Java-centric (Defects4J/GHRB/VUL4J).
