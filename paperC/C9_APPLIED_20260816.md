### S1 — `sections/03b_nulls_summary.tex`

This is the one the sweep flagged as the cost to raise with a co-author: a headline contribution
being presentationally demoted. It is written so a reader who never opens the appendix still learns
**what the four under-specifications are**, and it keeps every headline number.

> \subsection{Four under-specifications of the reference}
> \label{sec:nulls}
>
> A content floor is not a dataset constant. Four choices usually left implicit each move it by more
> than the effects under study, so all four are part of the measured construct and must be printed
> with the score. \emph{(i) The tie convention:} on MMLU-Pro, one dataset and one tokenizer give
> 0.125914 under \texttt{wrong} and 0.532164 under \texttt{credit}, a 40.6-point span whose upper end
> exceeds the intact base model's own \texttt{content\_norm} score by 32.5 points, and on
> ARC-Challenge \texttt{credit} alone moves five of six OLMo-2 arms from significantly above the
> floor to significantly below it. \emph{(ii) The length unit}, characters or continuation tokens:
> OpenBookQA \texttt{credit} is 0.416 versus 0.644, ARC-Challenge 18.60\% tied-longest under
> characters against 50.85\% under tokens. \emph{(iii) The tokenizer}, which moves \texttt{credit} by
> up to 10.6 points on four-way tasks and 9.26 on MMLU-Pro, non-monotonically in vocabulary size
> \citep{oostermeijer2026length}. \emph{(iv) The meaning of ``chance''} when \texttt{n\_opt} runs
> from three to ten: naive $1/10$ gives 0.100000 and $\mathrm{mean}(1/\texttt{n\_opt})$ gives
> 0.110877, so the gap to the 0.116606 always-A floor is either $+1.661$ points and $1.1661\times$ or
> $+0.573$ points and $1.0517\times$; both must be reported. The four are independent, so a token
> content floor is fixed only once all four are --- a property of
> $(\text{dataset},\text{convention},\text{unit},\text{tokenizer})$, not of the dataset. The letter
> floor is exempt from all four: a pure property of the item set's gold labels, invariant across all
> 15 cross-family arms and bit-identical across all 21 MMLU-Pro cells.
> Appendix~\ref{app:nulls} develops each with Tables~\ref{tab:nulls} and~\ref{tab:conventions}.

Writing notes: the numbered `(i)`–`(iv)` run-ins keep the *four-ness* explicit in the main text,
which is itself part of the argument that these are four **independent** degrees of freedom. I
tested a `\begin{itemize}` version of **exactly these words**; it reads better but the list structure
alone costs the paper its margin — measured, same wording, only the markup changed:

| S1 layout | extent | occupied pages | slack |
|---|---|---|---|
| numbered run-ins (shipped) | **8.857** | 9 | **109.8 pt** |
| `itemize`, identical wording | 8.996 | 9 | **0.0 pt** |

So the bullets are not merely more expensive, they land the paper in precisely the zero-slack family

### S2 — `sections/03_method.tex:50-59`

> \paragraph{Option-count-aware chance correction is decades old; only the varying-$k$ stratification is ours.}
> That a chance term should depend on how many options an item offers long predates this work
> \citep{bennett1954communications,brennan1981kappa,frary1988formula,brenner1996weightedkappa,%

> \emph{varies item to item} ($\texttt{n\_opt}\in\{3,\dots,10\}$, eight strata here), the permutation
> confined within strata so a letter illegal for an item can never be credited to it.
> Appendix~\ref{app:priorart} states that boundary claim by claim; its footprint is the $36$ items
> below.

### S5 — `sections/05_analysis.tex:25`

> Two qualifications belong with this two-reference comparison, both carried in
> Appendix~\ref{app:analysis-mult}. The exact McNemar instrument does not transfer to the chance

## 6. What I could NOT do, and what I want reviewed

### 6.1 The 9-page boundary is far sharper than "prose volume" suggests — measured

The 9-page result is **not** robust to a few extra words. Measured, by building each state:

| state | extent | occupied pages | slack |
|---|---|---|---|
| my prose, `exp` block at 728 B | 8.996 | 9 | **0.0 pt** |

| **my prose, `exp` block at 714 B** | **8.857** | **9** | **109.8 pt** |

**A 7-byte difference in one paragraph moves slack from 0.0 pt to 109.8 pt.** The metric is a step
function at the boundary, exactly as the sweep observed about `extent` saturating. Practical
consequence for whoever edits next: **adding even one clause anywhere in the main text will drop
this back into the zero-slack family**, which is the state the sweep rejected as "correct today and
fragile tomorrow". The 109.8 pt of slack is *not* 109.8 pt of editing room at the current
configuration — it is the reward for being just under a page-break threshold. Re-measure after any
edit; do not assume the slack absorbs it.

### 6.2 An early implementation error worth recording

My first application read 10 pages / extent 9.403 with real prose. I diagnosed it as "my prose is
too long" and spent four tightening passes on it. **That diagnosis was wrong.** A parity test —
substituting the sweep's verbatim draft stubs into *my* tree — still read 9.126 / 10 pages, proving
the deficit was **structural, not prose volume**. Diffing my tree against a rebuilt pristine `C9`
found the cause: `C9` also demotes `05_analysis.tex`'s `\subsection{Auditing the audit}` to a
`\paragraph{}`, which I had not replicated. After that one change the same parity test reproduced
`C9` exactly (8.857 / 109.8 pt), and the remaining gap really was prose volume.

Lesson for the next agent: when a relocation misses its measured target, **run the parity test
before rewriting prose** — swap the reference stubs into your own tree. If it still misses, the
problem is your structure, and no amount of tightening will fix it.
