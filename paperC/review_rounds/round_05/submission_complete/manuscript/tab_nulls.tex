\caption{Construct floors compared with chance, \emph{and with the sampling distribution of the
floor estimator itself}. Both percentage-point gaps and ratios are needed across option counts.
The last three columns calibrate the estimator. Because $\hat f=\max_L \hat m_L$ is a maximum over
$k$ noisy marginals estimated on a single finite item set, it is upward biased even when the gold
labels are exactly uniform, so a positive Gap is not by itself evidence that the floor differs from
chance. $\mathbb{E}[\hat f]$ and $q_{95}$ are the mean and 95th percentile of $\hat f$ under a
balanced null \emph{that the construct can actually realise}: the observed
$\texttt{n\_opt}$ histogram is held fixed and each item's gold letter is drawn uniformly among
\emph{its own} legal letters, so an item with four options is never assigned gold letter~J. Where
$\texttt{n\_opt}$ is constant this coincides exactly with drawing uniformly over all $k$ letters;
where it varies --- both MMLU-Pro rows, ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge --- it does not, and the
uniform-over-$k$ version we reported previously lies outside the support of any legal labelling
(Appendix~\ref{tab:app-construct-nulls} keeps that superseded column beside this one, and
Section~\ref{sec:legality-aware-null} states what it changed and in which direction).
$p=\Pr(\hat f\ge\text{observed floor})$ under the realisable null, evaluated at the floor's exact
rational value $\text{count}/n$ ($10^{6}$ draws per construct, three seeds, two independent
implementations; the reported $p<10^{-5}$ bound is the more conservative one carried over from the
earlier $2\times10^{5}$-draw run; evidence \textsf{E-CAL}, the single machine-readable source for
these rows). \textbf{Only MMLU and BoolQ have floors a balanced null could not plausibly produce}
($p<10^{-5}$); both are constructs with a genuinely fixed option count, so for them the choice of
null is not in question. For the six remaining letter constructs the observed floor lies inside the
estimator's own noise ($p=0.083$--$0.853$), and on CommonsenseQA and PIQA it is in fact
\emph{below} $\mathbb{E}[\hat f]$. Those six rows therefore support the weaker claim that chance is
the wrong \emph{reference}, while providing no evidence that the floor \emph{differs from} chance in
magnitude; the $+0.49$--$+2.60$~pp gaps there are the regime our own evidence record marks ``do not
oversell'' (evidence \textsf{E-A}). MMLU-Pro is the closest of the six to the conventional
threshold and should be read as unresolved at this $n$ rather than as settled either way. Floors and
chance values are the label-marginal floors re-derived first-hand from the raw per-item records
(evidence \textsf{E-A}; see the reproducibility statement for the artifact paths).}
