## PROSE REVIEW

### What this paper is, stripped of adjectives

The paper's argument has three moving parts, and it is worth separating them because they are not equally well supported.

1. **A reporting rule.** Before comparing arms, test each reported construct against its best constant, input-blind predictor. This is a normative proposal, and it is a good one.
2. **A claim that the rule bites.** The floor is not chance, and the floor-versus-chance choice reverses published readings: 14/15 designated damaged cells sit at or below the floor while reading above a chance line.
3. **A second, arm-conditional null (v2)** that is collapse-invariant, used to re-judge 27 existing cells.

Part 1 needs no experiment. Parts 2 and 3 are where the evidence lives, and both rest almost entirely on MMLU-Pro.

I want to record up front that this is an unusually honest manuscript. It runs a control against its own headline (the winner's-curse calibration) and lets that control narrow eight constructs to three. It voluntarily converts its most quotable number (10/12 versus 1/12) into a much less impressive one (3/12 versus 1/12) by holding both references to the same evidentiary standard, and it says in terms that seven of the ten "above chance" cells were never above chance by any tested standard. It refuses to state its own v1 ≥ v2 ordering as a theorem, gives the exact regularity condition, and constructs the emitter that violates it. It carries a 16-row retraction ledger naming its own withdrawn numbers. I reproduced the winner's-curse table independently and it is correct to Monte-Carlo error; I reproduced the κ identity, the 36-item ordering slack, the binomial 0.0196, and the ARC-Easy and keep14 deltas, and they are all exactly right. The internal arithmetic of this paper is in better shape than most submissions I see.

That makes the central problem more painful rather than less.

### The objection I think sinks the current version

**The balanced null used to calibrate MMLU-Pro's floor cannot produce MMLU-Pro data.**

Table 10 states the method explicitly: a "balanced (exactly uniform) multinomial null at each construct's own (n,k)", with k = 10 for MMLU-Pro. That draws each item's gold letter uniformly from A through J. But this paper's own Section A.1.4 is an argument that MMLU-Pro's `n_opt` runs from three to ten — the paper devotes a whole degree of freedom to it, and Appendix A.3 uses the eight strata to build its per-stratum argmax argument. On any item with fewer than ten options, letters beyond `n_opt` are illegal. No legal MMLU-Pro item set can have gold = J on a four-option item.

So every draw from the calibration null contains gold labels that no legal version of this benchmark could exhibit. Reconstructing an `n_opt` distribution consistent with the paper's own stated `mean(1/n_opt) = 0.110877` and its 623 mid-stratum items, about 10% of items have at least one illegal letter and roughly 5% of all drawn labels are illegal. The direction of the resulting bias is unambiguous and against the paper: spreading gold mass over ten letters when legal data concentrates it on fewer suppresses the null distribution of the maximum marginal, so the p-value is too small.

Replacing the null with the legal-support version — gold uniform over each item's *own* legal letters, everything else identical — I get p ≈ 0.061–0.085 across six plausible reconstructions of the `n_opt` distribution, against the paper's reported p < 10⁻⁵. Under the extremal least-skewing legal design (minimum occupancy in the short strata, bulk pushed to nine and ten options), p = 0.168 — the design most favourable to the paper is *worse* for it. And the mechanism is confirmed by a boundary check: if I illegally force nearly every item to ten options, the legal-support null converges onto the paper's own numbers to five decimal places (E[f̂] 0.104454 vs 0.104457; q₉₅ 0.107048 vs 0.107048) — but the implied `mean(1/n_opt)` becomes 0.1000. That is the diagnosis in one line: **the calibration null is silently anchored at naive 1/10 chance, the exact chance definition Section A.1.4 argues is the wrong one for this construct.** Measured against the item-average chance line the paper itself prefers, legal selection noise alone accounts for 55% of the reported +0.573 pp gap, and 75% under the extremal design.

Why this is structural rather than a table correction: MMLU-Pro is the only construct carrying the flip evidence. The 14/15 count, the 21 powered cells, the 3/12-versus-1/12 comparison, and all 27 v2 cells are MMLU-Pro. The five constructs the paper *already* concedes are inside estimator noise are the very ARC-*/OpenBookQA/CommonsenseQA/PIQA benchmarks carrying the off-MMLU 10/15 and 0/60 counts. If MMLU-Pro's floor is not distinguishable from what a null would produce, the only surviving calibrated constructs are MMLU (k = 4) and BoolQ (k = 2) — and neither carries a single headline count. The paper's abstract says it "rest[s] the quantitative form of this claim on the first three". Remove the first of those three and the quantitative claim has no benchmark left that also demonstrates the flip.

Two things this does *not* damage, and I want to be exact. MMLU and BoolQ have fixed k, so legal support equals balanced support; I confirmed their p < 10⁻⁵ is untouched. And the floor value 0.116606 itself is a correct empirical label marginal — nothing about the v1 statistic is wrong. What collapses is specifically the claim that this floor is distinguishable from a null-generated floor.

I am also explicit about the limit of my own attack. I do not have the per-item `n_opt` vector; it is not in the snapshot. My p-values come from reconstructions, not from the real distribution. I therefore assert "the null lies outside the legal support, and every reconstruction I could build puts p above 0.05", not "p = 0.08". The fix is cheap — the authors hold the `n_opt` column, and re-running the calibration is CPU-seconds — and it is the single item that should decide this paper.

There is a real irony worth stating plainly: this paper's own thesis is that a reference outside the support of legal outcomes is not a reference. Its most sophisticated control violates that thesis. Fixing it is more in the paper's spirit than defending it would be.

### The second-strongest objection: a missing arm in the headline denominator

Appendix A.4 promises that "any damaged arm excluded from that denominator is named at the point of exclusion together with its own floor delta, so that no ratio is computed over an undisclosed subset." Section 4 lists OLMo-2's damaged arms as keep8, keep10, keep12, keep14, **and shortgpt16**. But the 14/15 counts only three OLMo-2 MMLU-Pro cells, and I could not find shortgpt16 named as excluded anywhere in the 24-page PDF.

It is not a borderline omission. From the paper's own Table 9 accuracy of 0.153341 against the always-A floor, shortgpt16 sits **+3.674 pp above the floor** — about sixteen times the size of the one acknowledged exception (qwen3/k14 at +0.233 pp) — and its v2 verdict is "item-level signal" at p = 0.0001 with Δ_perm = +4.054 pp. It clears *both* nulls decisively, including the arm-conditional gate the paper proposes as the stronger test. Restoring it makes 14/15 into 14/16, and turns "one statistically real but materially negligible exception" into two exceptions of which one is large and material.

I cannot rule out a defensible construction-based reason for the exclusion — shortgpt16 is a different pruning method, so arguably not a depth "rung". But the paper wrote the disclosure rule itself, and the one arm where honouring it would cost something is the one arm where it was not honoured. In a paper whose entire pitch is that denominators and references must be stated explicitly, that is a major issue rather than a minor one.

### Where the statistics are thinner than they read

The paper concedes that under BH or Bonferroni neither reference retains a cell (0/12 both sides) and then rescues an inferential claim from the count: P(X ≥ 3 | Bin(12, 0.05)) = 0.0196. I verified that value exactly. But it is one cell from vanishing — P(X ≥ 2) = 0.1184 — and this is the *only* surviving inferential statement about the chance side. Worse, the binomial calculation assumes independence, and Appendix A.4 has just argued at length that these cells "share items, nest arms, and share a null, so the tests are neither independent nor exchangeable". The paper uses dependence to decline BH, then relies on a statistic requiring independence. Positive dependence inflates the joint-rejection probability, so 0.0196 is likely anti-conservative as well as fragile. An item-level resampling of the 12-cell rejection count would settle it and needs no new measurement.

The parallel "0/60 damaged cells clear their floor" aggregate is offered as near-unanimity. Under an exact zero-effect null, P(0 of 60) = 0.219 at a two-sided 0.05 rule, with 1.5 clears expected. Combined with the paper's own statement that 52/60 are underpowered, 0/60 is consistent with the claim but is not strong evidence for it, and should not be described as if the count itself were surprising.

Finally, the materiality bar that disposes of qwen3/k14 is cleared by 0.005 — recovery_fraction 0.049 against a bar of 0.0538 implied by the paper's own "9.1% of the anchor" — with no interval reported on a ratio of two estimated quantities. The 0.10 constant's pre-registration I accept; the absence of uncertainty around the statistic compared to it I do not.

### Scope versus advertisement

"Four model families" reads as four replications. Section 4 discloses that it is one prune-then-heal family (3 cells) plus three families sharing a single evaluation-time front-N truncation operator (12 cells) with "no fresh block, optimizer, gradient, healing, or damaged checkpoint". Twelve of fifteen headline cells come from one crude intervention that is close to guaranteed to destroy a letter read-out.

The paper does label this — but the label is scoped to identifying a *family effect*, which is not the claim the abstract makes. The abstract claims the flip generalises across four families, and no stated limitation binds that. This is the test the adversarial lens asks for: delete the most-hedged sentence in Section 7 and nothing a reader takes from the abstract changes. That is a self-limitation that does not bind.

### Provenance

One avoidable wound: the shipped `build_record.json` attests to a PDF of 355196 bytes, sha256 `56a376e1…`, 22 pages. The shipped PDF is 366583 bytes, sha256 `1fbaaf99…`, and 24 pages by direct measurement. Timestamps (record 06:48, PDF 10:13) suggest an innocent late rebuild, and I found no substantive inconsistency in the PDF. But a paper arguing that every number must be mechanically bound to a record should not ship an attestation disagreeing with its artifact on all three fields. The record's own `pdf_visually_inspected: false` cites the absence of a PDF rasteriser; PyMuPDF is present in this repository's environment — I used it — so that gate looks closeable today.

Citation integrity I could check only internally: all 17 keys resolve, all 17 render, none uncited, zero undefined citations in the build log. Whether the fifteen substantive references support their local sentences is **unchecked** — I had no network access. Several venue strings are of the kind that need family-specific verification, and the paper itself flags that the Cho et al. camera-ready could not be diffed. I flag this as unverified, not defective.

### Verdict

The strongest objection is ADV-1, and I judge it **fatal to the current version but not to the work**. Fatal because it removes the calibrated status of the only construct that carries the headline; not fatal to the work because the repair is arithmetic the authors can run today from data they already hold, and because *either* outcome leaves something publishable. If the legal-support p stays below 0.05, the paper is essentially intact and worth 6.5–7. If it does not, the honest reframing — "we tried to establish that reported floors exceed chance, and after calibrating the floor estimator against its own noise on its own legal support, almost none of them demonstrably do" — is a genuine and useful negative methodological result, worth about 6 with the abstract and both null tables restricted accordingly.

What I cannot do is score the paper as it currently stands as if the calibration were sound, because its central quantitative claim is compared against a reference that cannot generate the data. Combined with the undisclosed shortgpt16 exclusion (ADV-2) and the one-cell margin on the sole multiplicity-surviving statistic (ADV-3), that puts this at **5 — weak reject**: promising, methodologically serious, more honest than most of its competition, and one CPU-minute of recomputation away from being decidable either way.

My ceiling without new experiments is **5.5**, because ADV-1's resolution requires recomputation the snapshot does not contain — though I stress that this recomputation is *not* a new experiment, and if it were shipped in a revision my ceiling would rise immediately.

The single change that would most improve my confidence: recompute the MMLU-Pro rows of E-CAL under a null whose support is the legal one, using the real per-item `n_opt` vector, and propagate the result honestly wherever it lands.
