## PROSE REVIEW

### Summary of my assessment

This is a careful, unusually self-critical measurement paper making a modest and correct central point: a multiple-choice score should be tested against the construct's best constant, input-blind predictor before it is compared with another arm, and nominal chance is frequently the wrong reference. The paper's most valuable technical move is not the floor itself (majority-class baselines are old, and the paper says so) but the observation that **the floor is a maximum over k noisy label marginals and therefore needs its own calibration** — a winner's-curse correction that, when applied, forces the authors to withdraw the quantitative form of their headline on five of eight constructs. That is real methodological work, and I verified it reproduces exactly.

I recommend weak accept. My reservations are concentrated in one place, and it is the place my lens is aimed at: **the calibration that the paper's credibility rests on is, for its most important benchmark, computed under a null that violates the paper's own stated principle.** That is issue S-01 and it is the reason I cannot score technical soundness above 3.

### The central soundness problem (S-01)

The paper's identity is built on a distinction it draws sharply and repeatedly: a letter that is *illegal* for an item must never be credited to it. This is the sole novelty claim for read-out v2 — "the permutation confined within strata so a letter illegal for an item can never be credited to it" — and it is the stated reason the stratification is "load-bearing."

The balanced-null calibration in Table 1 does not respect that principle. Its method string says "balanced (exactly uniform) multinomial null at each construct's own (n,k)", and the generated table prints **k = 10** for both MMLU-Pro rows. But MMLU-Pro's `n_opt` runs from 3 to 10 across eight strata — the paper says so twice, and makes an entire under-specification subsection out of the fact that "chance" is ambiguous because of it. Drawing gold labels uniformly over ten letters for all 12,032 items credits illegal letters on the null side, inflates $k$, pulls each marginal toward $1/10$, and suppresses the variance of the maximum. The observed floor is compared against a reference distribution that is too tight.

I quantified this. First I confirmed I could reproduce the paper exactly — all nine rows of Table 1, every $\mathbb{E}[\hat f]$, every $q_{95}$, every $p$, to all printed digits, at the stated seed. Then I recomputed the MMLU-Pro rows under a legality-respecting null in which each item's gold label is uniform over *that item's own* legal letters. I do not have the per-item `n_opt` vector, so I constrained it using only statistics the paper itself reports — $\mathrm{mean}(1/\texttt{n\_opt}) = 0.110877$, eight non-empty strata, and the 623 items in strata 5–8 from the ordering appendix — and swept the residual mass across every admissible allocation:

| null | $\mathbb{E}[\hat f]$ | $q_{95}$ | $p$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| paper's nominal $k=10$ | 0.104457 | 0.107048 | $<10^{-5}$ |
| legality-respecting (excess → 3-way) | 0.113452 | 0.116938 | 0.068 |
| legality-respecting (excess → 4-way) | 0.114013 | 0.117271 | 0.093 |
| legality-respecting (excess → 9-way) | 0.114491 | 0.117603 | 0.118 |

Under **every** allocation, $q_{95}$ reaches or exceeds the observed floor of 0.116606 and $p$ lands in the 0.07–0.12 band, stable across three seeds. MMLU-Pro would move from "above balanced null" to "inside estimator noise" — the very partition the authors created in order to *stop* resting quantitative claims on such constructs. Since MMLU ($k=4$) and BoolQ ($k=2$) have fixed option counts and are untouched, "only three of the eight letter constructs" becomes two, and the abstract's parenthetical loses its first member.

I want to be precise about severity. This is **major, not fatal**. The paper's actual rule — state and calibrate your reference — survives intact; arguably this finding *vindicates* it. The wrong-null flip does not depend on this $p$-value. MMLU and BoolQ still clear the calibration. But MMLU-Pro is described as "the cleanest test," and it carries the 21 powered cells, the 14/15 headline, and the entire v2 re-analysis. One of three surviving quantitative pillars is, on the paper's own logic, not surviving. And the failure mode is the specific one the paper was written to warn about, which makes it more consequential than the arithmetic alone suggests.

### What I verified and what held

I want to be equally clear that most of what I checked was correct, sometimes impressively so.

- **Table 1's calibration**: all nine rows reproduce to every printed digit. This is the paper's central quantity and the computation is sound.
- **The $\kappa$ identity**: $\Delta_{\mathrm{perm}} = \kappa(1-p_e)$ holds exactly on all ten rows I tested. The paper's disclaimer that the statistic is textbook is accurate, and it says so more plainly than most authors would.
- **The ordering arithmetic**: $f_{\mathrm{const}} = 1403/12032 = 0.116606$; the per-stratum bound $1439/12032 = 0.119598$; the gap of **exactly 36 items = 0.2992 pp**. The paper correctly declines to state the ordering as a theorem and supplies the explicit counterexample.
- **Cross-table consistency**: all eleven v1 deltas in `tab_mmlupro` are exactly recoverable as $100(\mathrm{Acc} - 0.116606)$ from `tab_v2_full`'s accuracies. So is `olmo2/keep14`'s $+0.324$. The 10/12 count, $P(X\geq3\mid 12, 0.05) = 0.0196$, the BH and Bonferroni 0/12, $2532/14042 = 18.03\%$, and the ARC-Easy $+38.76$ pp gap all reproduce.
- **Citations — the strongest dimension.** Arcon et al. reports exactly the claimed pattern. Bean et al. contains **exactly 27 checklist items** (I extracted and counted them) and its text has **zero** occurrences of "majority", "null model" or "constant" — the paper's load-bearing negative claim is correct. OLMES says verbatim "we standardize to evaluate each model using both the MCF and CF formulations, and the best performing one is used", and uses "random baseline" with zero hits for "chance", confirming both the description and the corrective "this is not a size-keyed rule." Feng 2019 supports the specific necessary-not-sufficient use. I found no fabricated or misdirected citation.

The symmetric re-test deserves particular credit. Holding both references to the same two-sided interval standard shrinks the authors' own headline from 10/12-vs-1/12 to 3/12-vs-1/12, and they state that seven of the ten "above chance" cells were never above chance by any tested standard. Authors who wanted a bigger number would not have run that test.

### Secondary findings

**S-02** is a confirmed arithmetic error refuted by the paper against itself: $0.532164/0.125914 = 4.226$, not the stated $4.6\times$. Both operands are in the paper's own table. Its significance is diagnostic — the Reproducibility Statement claims a checker resolves all 610 numerals as exact matches, roundings, or *differences*, and a **ratio** is none of those. This was the one printed ratio I could recompute from shipped values, and it failed; the 2.11×/0.90×/0.98× residual ratios remain unverifiable.

**S-03** concerns the aggregate the paper twice says its conclusions rest on. "0/60 damaged cells clear their floor" is offered as the near-unanimity justifying no family-wise correction — but the paper's own MMLU-Pro result is 14/15, i.e. one designated damaged cell *does* clear its floor at $p=0.0192$. The 0/60 must be a disjoint off-MMLU set, yet neither passage says so, no union is reported, and the decomposition is short by one cell ($7 + 52 = 59$). The paper commits explicitly to naming every exclusion; here it does not. I could also not reconstruct the off-MMLU 15 from five arms × five benchmarks.

The remainder are minor: an unverifiable materiality step where the intact-Qwen3 anchor is never printed and the one anchor supplied gives 90% rather than 9.1% (**S-04**); length-unit ranges that do not match their table, with the upper end set by the *negative control* (**S-05**); a "21 cells" set that is never enumerated and a 0.968 half-width appearing in no table (**S-06**); a build record certifying a different PDF than the one shipped — 22 pages and 355,196 bytes versus the actual 24 pages and 366,583 (**S-07**); a hand-vs-generated table disagreement on CommonsenseQA, 0.885 vs 0.884 (**S-08**); an inventory count of "ten constructs" not reconstructible from the stated lists (**S-09**); and a post-hoc materiality constant whose 0.10 bar the dismissed counterexample misses by 9% relative, with no sensitivity reported (**S-10**).

### Decision-relevant answers

1. **Strongest verified contribution.** The winner's-curse calibration of the best-constant floor, which reproduces exactly and forces a genuine self-narrowing.
2. **Most severe unresolved issue.** S-01: the MMLU-Pro balanced null credits illegal letters, and correcting it moves MMLU-Pro into the estimator's own noise band.
3. **Weakest evidence-to-importance ratio.** The "0/60" aggregate (S-03) — load-bearing for the entire multiplicity stance, yet not reconcilable with the 14/15 headline and internally short a cell.
4. **Score ceiling without new experiments: 7.** Every issue I raise is fixable by recomputation and rewriting from data the authors already hold; none requires a new model run. S-01 needs only the per-item `n_opt` vector.
5. **Single change that would most improve confidence.** Recompute the MMLU-Pro calibration under a legality-respecting null and let the construct partition follow the result, whichever way it falls.
6. **What would lower the score.** Discovering additional undisclosed above-floor cells in the union, an unproducible 21-cell set, or further failing ratios — any of which would undermine the mechanical-binding claim on which this paper's pitch depends.

### A note on the paper's epistemic character

I reviewed this adversarially and it mostly held. The retraction ledger, the "auditing the audit" section, the refusal to state the ordering as a theorem, the disclosure that the v2 numbers are post-hoc by construction, and the symmetric re-test that shrinks the authors' own headline are all things a paper optimising for acceptance would omit. The irony of S-01 is that it is not a lapse of care but a lapse of *reach*: the authors identified illegal-letter crediting as the central threat, built v2 to close it on the statistic side, and left it open on the calibration side. Fixing it will cost them their strongest benchmark's $p$-value and make the paper more credible, not less. On the evidence in front of me the paper sits just above the accept boundary, and would sit comfortably above it with S-01, S-02 and S-03 addressed.
