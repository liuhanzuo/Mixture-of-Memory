\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Recovery varies across knowledge domains}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.86\textwidth]{figures/fig_domain_recovery.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{MMLU recovery is heterogeneous across broad domains.}
Accuracy is sample weighted over the standard four MMLU groups. Recovery is
chance adjusted, $(a-0.25)/(a_{\mathrm{base}}-0.25)$. Full32 remains close to
the base in every group; ShortGPT consistently exceeds keep14, but neither
half-depth arm closes the intact-model gap.}
\label{fig:domain-recovery}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:domain-recovery} separates absolute accuracy from recovery
relative to each group's base margin. keep14 is strongest on the heterogeneous
Other group (.377; 29.1\% chance-adjusted recovery) and weakest on STEM (.294;
15.6\%) and Humanities (.299; 16.2\%). Social Science has higher raw accuracy
(.335) but only 18.6\% recovery because its base is much stronger. ShortGPT
improves every group, most notably Other (.574) and Social Science (.554), yet
still remains below full32.

Subject-level examples show that the pattern is not a single-domain artifact.
Among high-sample subjects, keep14 retains relatively more on miscellaneous
($n{=}783$: .457 versus .803 base) and marketing ($n{=}234$: .440 versus .825),
but little on professional law ($n{=}1{,}534$: .272 versus .460) and high-school
statistics ($n{=}216$: .213 versus .514). These examples are descriptive: group
membership mixes base difficulty, answer format, corpus exposure, factual recall,
and reasoning. They do not establish that a particular knowledge type is stored
in the removed blocks.

\subsection{Observed trajectories differ across two prefix arms}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_keep8_falsification.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The shallow keep8 arm improves in likelihood and ordinary tasks
through 44k.} The retained 44k and 121k aggregate checkpoints both remain near
MMLU chance; the comparison is descriptive because the available run lacks
seed replication.}
\label{fig:keep8}
\end{figure}

keep8 and keep14 provide complementary within-arm trajectories. keep8's PPL
falls smoothly while aggregate MMLU changes from .2463 at 44k to .2535 at 121k;
keep14 gains 1.79 points from .3012 at 128k to .3191 at 200k while PPL improves
by 0.265 (Figure~\ref{fig:keep14-trajectory}). A common per-item rerun estimates
the keep14 change as 1.68 points (95\% CI $[1.08,2.29]$). These intervals are
conditional on the realized checkpoints, and the two arms do not share a common
budget, starting quality, or seed design. The supported claim is only that the
two observed trajectories differ; they do not identify an inherited-depth
effect or eventual convergence.

\subsection{A coupled 16-layer construction comparison}
\input{sections/tab_policy_endpoint}

ShortGPT and keep14 end at 16 blocks and complete the same 200k target budget,
but differ along four coupled dimensions: ShortGPT inherits 16 rather than 14
pretrained blocks, selects them non-contiguously, retains original block 31, and
uses no fresh tail. keep14 retains a contiguous prefix and appends two randomly
initialized blocks. Embeddings, final normalization, and output heads are copied
from the same base and therefore do not explain the difference.

The resulting single-run endpoint gap is large: ShortGPT reaches .474 MMLU
versus .319 for keep14 while also obtaining lower PPL. Thus these two
constructions show that nominal depth alone does not fix a unique observed
endpoint. The comparison does \emph{not} show
that block 31 is uniquely responsible, that non-contiguous selection is optimal,
or that fresh tails are harmful. Additional matched structures would be needed
to test these hypotheses separately.

\subsection{Scope checks, not replications}
A more compressed OLMo-2-1B arm exhibits the same qualitative pattern of falling
PPL with near-chance MMLU, providing same-family context rather than an
independent replication. An existing Qwen3-8B endpoint supplies a limited
cross-family check: a 14/36-layer prefix+fresh-tail model remains at .2495 MMLU
after 200k steps, compared with .7297 for its base (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:app-crossfamily}). Qwen retains a smaller depth fraction than the
OLMo keep14 recipe (39\% versus 44\%), which is one plausible contributor to its
larger deficit, but the corpus, architecture, and missing content/closed-book
controls prevent attribution. We therefore treat Qwen as directional evidence,
not a matched replication.
