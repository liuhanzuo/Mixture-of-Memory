\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental setup}
All main results use OLMo-2-7B under the base-model protocol in
\S\ref{sec:method}. The central
comparisons are the intact base, full32 continued-pretraining control,
keep14+fresh2, ShortGPT-16, frozen-front, and fully random-init operating
points. Detailed per-task results and all available checkpoints are in
Appendix~\ref{app:complete-results}.

\subsection{Main result: the observed axes remain separated}
\input{sections/tab_main_results}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} gives the central measurement. In the single
half-depth keep14 run, the observed endpoint
reaches PPL 10.561 after 200k steps---a large improvement, but still
$1.428\times$ the base---and remains far below the base on both MMLU interfaces
and all three closed-book tasks. The intact full32 branch remains near the base
at its available step-25k checkpoint in held-out likelihood, MMLU, and
closed-book evaluation. This argues against short-horizon corpus shift; it does
not identify what would happen to the intact model through 200k steps.

The MMLU interface matters but does not erase the result. Scoring complete option
text raises keep14's above-chance recovery relative to answer-letter scoring,
which is consistent with a content-to-symbol or readout contribution. Yet
fully random initialization attains nearly the same normalized-content score as
frozen-front despite chance-level answer letters. The content protocol therefore
contains a large fluency floor; neither interface alone is identified with
stored knowledge.

The separation is not specific to multiple choice. At 200k, keep14 reaches only
.142 PopQA answer containment, .294 TriviaQA exact match, and .060 NQ-open exact
match, compared with .257, .636, and .205 for the intact base. These factual
recall tasks use a shared no-retrieval generation interface, while full32
continued pretraining preserves much more of the base performance. Together with
the two MMLU protocols, they argue against an answer-letter-only explanation.

Figure~\ref{fig:ppl-mmlu-scatter} makes the measurement issue visible. PPL and
MMLU broadly co-vary, and no compressed arm reaches base PPL. Within these
heterogeneous operating points, however, PPL alone does not recover the full
ordering: random-init has lower PPL than frozen-front but lower MMLU. Because
learning rate, trainable modules, structure, and checkpoint budget vary, this is
a descriptive counterexample to a one-number summary, not a matched-quality
estimate or evidence of global uncorrelatedness.

\subsection{Controlled comparisons}
\paragraph{Same-shape initialization and adaptation.}
At 200k, keep14, frozen-front, and fully random init share the 16-layer shape.
Their metric rankings disagree: frozen-front has worse PPL than random init but
higher answer-letter MMLU, while train-all keep14 is strongest on both. Paired
item-level tests on a common rerun quantify the keep14 advantage over each
same-shape operating point, but learning
rate and trainable-module differences prevent an initialization-only or
adaptation-only causal claim. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app-paired} reports exact
McNemar tests and paired-bootstrap intervals.

\paragraph{Full-model corpus control.}
Through the available 25k checkpoint, continuing the intact 32-layer
model leaves PPL and both MMLU interfaces close to the original base and
preserves most PopQA, TriviaQA, and NQ-open performance. Thus short-horizon
corpus shift is insufficient to explain the large half-depth gap. This control
is not step- or token-exposure-matched to keep14 at 200k, so we do not treat it
as an exact long-budget counterfactual.

\paragraph{Scoring-interface diagnostic.}
Across nine evaluated checkpoints or operating points, complete-option MMLU is
less depth sensitive than
answer-letter MMLU. ShortGPT remains stronger than the prefix arms under both
interfaces, showing that the observed endpoint differs across constructions.
The comparison changes prompt and candidate scoring together and has no paired
uncertainty analysis, so it is exploratory rather than a clean interface
ablation. The full table and protocol definition are in Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:app-protocol-controls}.
