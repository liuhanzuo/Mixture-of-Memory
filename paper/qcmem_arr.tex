\begin{abstract}
Processing contexts far beyond a backbone's native window remains costly:
full attention grows quadratically and, past the extrapolation limit,
simply collapses. We introduce \textbf{QCMem} (Query-Conditioned Memory),
which recasts long-context processing from \emph{token-partial} KV reuse
(recompute the full-depth KV of a subset of tokens) to
\emph{layer-partial} readout: split the transformer at depth $j$, cache the
single depth-$j$ residual-stream hidden of each chunk, then at read time
retrieve the top-$k$ relevant chunks and recompute only the upper layers
$[j{:}L]$ over a fixed-size pack. The read cost and memory are independent of
context length (constant $\approx$6.7k tokens / $\approx$18\,GB for an 8B
backbone). Across five long-context benchmarks (RULER, BABILong, LongEval,
LongBench, LoCoMo) with three mechanism-level baselines under an identical
harness, QCMem matches full-context accuracy inside the window and is
\emph{the only method that still works past the backbone's extrapolation
limit}: at 128k, full-context / full-KV recompute collapse to 0 while QCMem
stays at RULER~100 and LongEval~0.98, with a $7.8\times$ prefill speedup and
constant memory. We attribute the effectiveness to an
understand-then-generate division of labor---semantic content saturates in
mid layers---and give both correlational probes and a causal end-to-end test.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Long-context inference is bottlenecked twice. First, full self-attention is
$O(L^2)$ in prefill and $O(L)$ in KV memory. Second, and more fundamentally,
once the context exceeds the backbone's RoPE extrapolation limit, accuracy
does not merely degrade---it \emph{collapses}. On Qwen3-8B (native window
$40{,}960$; the config's $131{,}072$ is an un-enabled YaRN ceiling), feeding
128k tokens drives RULER needle accuracy to $0$.

Retrieval-augmented and KV-reuse methods attack the first bottleneck but
inherit the second: they still recompute or attend over a length that scales
with the context. We instead make both the read computation \emph{and} the
memory constant in context length. The idea is a \emph{depth partition}: for a
transformer of $L$ layers split at depth $j$,
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Write} each 512-token chunk through the bottom layers $[0{:}j]$
and cache its depth-$j$ residual-stream hidden $h_j$ (chunk-local RoPE).
Storage is $\sim$$1/(2L)$ of a full KV cache.
\item \textbf{Read} by retrieving the top-$k$ chunks (BM25), packing
\texttt{[sink; selected $h_j$; query $h_j$]}, and recomputing only the upper
layers $[j{:}L]$ under fresh RoPE to produce logits. The pack is a fixed
$\sim$6.7k tokens regardless of context length.
\end{itemize}
The split depth $j$ is a knob: $j{=}0$ is full RAG recompute (exact-equals the
full forward, $\max|\Delta\text{logit}|<10^{-4}$), $j{=}L$ is closed-book. Our
contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{A new primitive} (\S\ref{sec:method}): recomputing $[j{:}L]$ as a
\emph{readout} over a retrieved, layer-partial cache; the first framing of
long-context memory as depth-partitioned rather than token-partial.
\item \textbf{Five-benchmark, three-baseline evidence} (\S\ref{sec:results})
under one harness, showing constant-cost QCMem matches full-context inside the
window and is uniquely usable past the extrapolation limit.
\item \textbf{A mechanistic account} (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}): probing plus a
causal end-to-end test that a compressible mid-depth hidden is what makes the
readout work.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method: QCMem}
\label{sec:method}
Let a decoder-only LM have layers $[0{:}L]$. QCMem fixes a split depth $j$.

\paragraph{Write.} The document is chunked into 512-token spans. Each chunk is
run through $[0{:}j]$ with chunk-local positions, and its depth-$j$
residual-stream hidden $h_j\in\mathbb{R}^{512\times d}$ is stored. Because only
the bottom $j$ layers run and only one hidden state per token is kept, storage
is $\approx$$1/(2L)$ of a full-depth KV cache and write is $O(L_{\text{ctx}})$
but shallow.

\paragraph{Read.} Given a query, BM25 retrieves the top-$k$ chunks. We form the
pack \texttt{[sink; $h_j$ of selected chunks; $h_j$ of the query]}, assign
\emph{fresh} contiguous RoPE positions, and recompute layers $[j{:}L]$ with
full attention over the pack to obtain next-token logits. The pack length is
$\text{sink}+k\cdot\text{chunk}+\text{query}\approx6.7$k for $k{=}12$,
chunk $512$---\emph{independent of context length}.

\paragraph{The $j$ knob.} At $j{=}0$ the read recomputes all layers over the
retrieved raw tokens; a self-test confirms this equals the full forward to
$\max|\Delta\text{logit}|<10^{-4}$. At $j{=}L$ nothing is recomputed
(closed-book). Intermediate $j$ trades cache compressibility against readout
fidelity.

\paragraph{Training.} A student at $j{=}12$ is trained by self-distillation
(LoRA $r{=}32,\alpha{=}64$, 4000 steps, 8-GPU DDP) against a $j{=}0$ teacher on
plain PG19 continuous windows---\emph{no synthetic long-context data}. This
pushes the usable cache depth from $j{\le}9$ to $j{\ge}12$.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:results}
\paragraph{Setup.} Unless noted, the backbone is Qwen3-8B, chunk size 512,
split $j{=}12$. All three baselines share the identical harness, backbone, and
scorer---the only variable is the tested primitive:
\textbf{KV-Direct}~\citep{residualstream} forces $j{=}0$ (full-depth
recompute), no retrieval, keeps all tokens---mathematically the full-context
accuracy (self-test $\max$ diff $<10^{-4}$), only caching the residual stream
to save memory; \textbf{HCache}~\citep{hcache} caches mid-layer activations and
recomputes upper layers post-hoc, but with no retrieval and no training;
\textbf{MemoryLLM} is a dedicated fixed-size memory model (same-class baseline).
Synthetic tasks use official \texttt{string\_match} scoring; BABILong uses the
official \texttt{compare\_answers} (not \texttt{re.search}); QA tasks use
official F1. Retrieval $k$ is set per task (long-context retrieval has a
task-dependent optimum; a fixed default systematically underestimates QCMem).

\subsection{Efficiency}
Table~\ref{tab:eff} (median-of-3). QCMem prefill is write $O(L)$ plus a fixed

recomputing $[j{:}L]$ over the pack), which is optimizable with a resumed-band
KV cache.

\subsection{Real long-document and dialogue QA}
On BABILong (Table~\ref{tab:overview}), QCMem is strongest on relational qa5
(8k/16k/32k = 79/67/63) while KV-Direct wins single-fact localization qa1---an
honest boundary. On LongBench, all three methods score 4--12 F1 (Qwen3-8B base,
no chat template; the task is intrinsically hard), and QCMem's AVG 9.58 tracks
KV-Direct's 10.13 within noise using a \emph{constant} read---so the low
absolute score reflects task difficulty, not a QCMem weakness. On LoCoMo,
QCMem (F1 9.05 / acc 24.1) $\approx$ KV-Direct (8.72 / 20.3) and both far
exceed HCache (4.73 / 6.4). On LongEval lines-retrieval, with the per-task
optimum $k{\in}\{4,6\}$, QCMem matches inside the window
(8k/16k/32k = 1.00/0.96/1.00 vs.\ KV-Direct 1.00/0.96/0.92) and \emph{reverses
sharply past it} (64k 0.94 vs.\ 0.34; 128k 0.98 vs.\ 0.00).

\subsection{Ablations}
\paragraph{Chunk size.} Read length $=k\cdot\text{chunk}+\text{sink}+\text{query}$
scales with chunk. Chunk 512 is the sweet spot: strong prefill speedup
($7.83\times$ at 128k), low flat memory (17--18\,GB), moderate decode (2.4\,s),
and high multikey (128k$=$94)---near chunk-1024 accuracy at $\approx$half the
decode cost.
\paragraph{Cross-chunk attention.} Recomputing $[j{:}L]$ with full attention
vs.\ block-diagonal (reuse per-chunk KV, no cross-chunk): identical on single
needle (100$=$100, one chunk suffices) but a large gap on multikey
(88/92 vs.\ 44/40, $\Delta{+}44/{+}52$) and BABILong qa2/qa5. Cross-chunk
recompute is load-bearing for multi-fact disambiguation, degradable to save
compute on single-needle tasks.

\section{Why caching a mid-layer hidden works}
\label{sec:mechanism}
\paragraph{Division of labor.} Probing shows semantic-task accuracy peaks in
mid layers and falls at the top, while next-token ability forms only in the top
layers (consistent across Qwen and Llama). Causally, using only the front
$\sim$4--8 layers (depth 0.12--0.22) reaches 95\% of the full model's
downstream semantics. The cacheable depth ceiling ($j{\le}9$ zero-shot, pushed
to $j{\ge}12$ by self-distillation) coincides with this semantic-saturation
point.
\paragraph{Caching the top fails.} A negative control (caching a top-layer
hidden) collapses: the top hidden is a query-sensitive, pre-readout
representation, so caching it discards query conditioning
(qa5 $(12,0){=}61/50 \to (12,6){=}29/20$). Only caching the query-insensitive
bottom-$j$ hidden is safe---validating the front-$j$ design.
\paragraph{Causal end-to-end test.} Pretraining a model with a low-rank funnel
at layer $j$ (which forces the depth-$j$ hidden to be compressible) yields a
representation that degrades \emph{less} under rank-$r$ PCA compression of the
cache than a vanilla model at every rank, and the advantage \emph{grows with
model size} (1B$\to$3B $\approx 2\times$). This is direct evidence that a
compressible mid-depth hidden---not an incidental property---is what the
readout exploits.

\section{Related Work}
QCMem is a new combination of known components plus one new primitive
(recompute $[j{:}L]$ as readout). The closest prior work:
\textbf{HCache}~\citep{hcache} caches mid-layer activations and recomputes
upper layers in a serving system, but post-hoc, untrained, and without
retrieval; we show head-to-head that this inference form collapses on long
context (8k$=$34, 16k$=$2). \textbf{KV-Direct}~\citep{residualstream} caches
the residual stream and recomputes full-depth KV, training-free, keeping all
tokens; we show it equals full-context accuracy but collapses at 128k
($=0$) with an exploding read length---QCMem's layer-partial{+}retrieval{+}fixed
read is the delta. Token-axis KV compression (H2O, SnapKV, StreamingLLM,
CompressKV) and continued-pretraining compressibility (KV-CAT) are orthogonal
to our depth-axis partition. Ultra-long anchors: YOCO, InfLLM.

\section{Limitations}
QCMem is an efficiency / extrapolation method, not an accuracy SOTA: within the
backbone's extrapolation window full-context is $\ge$ QCMem (notably
variable-tracking, 64k 98 vs.\ 21). The watershed shifts with the backbone's
extrapolation ability---weaker backbones benefit earlier and more. Multi-hop
variable-tracking is weak; single/double-fact localization (BABILong qa1/qa2)
inside the window favors KV-Direct. The head-to-head sweep is an $n{=}50$ cut
(vs.\ $n{=}100$ elsewhere) and the topk$\times$length grid is sparse. Decode is
slower than full attention and is future work (resumed-band KV cache).
